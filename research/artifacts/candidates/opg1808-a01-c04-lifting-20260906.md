## Lemma 1: lifting from a genuine uniform quotient

Let V(T) be partitioned into nonempty parts B_i. Suppose, for each distinct
i,j, every cross-arc between B_i and B_j has the same direction and colour.
Let Q be the resulting coloured quotient tournament. Suppose i is a monosource
of Q and v is a monosource of the induced tournament T[B_i].
Then v is a monosource of T. There is NO two-colour restriction on Q.

Proof. For a target y in B_i, use its monochromatic path from v inside B_i.
For y in B_j with j != i, choose a monochromatic directed path
i=i_0 -> i_1 -> ... -> i_t=j in Q, of some colour c. Its vertices may be taken
distinct: repetitions in a monochromatic walk can be removed without changing
its colour. Choose one arbitrary representative in every intermediate part,
start at v, and end at the actual target y. Each successive cross-arc has
direction forward and colour c by the uniformity assumption. Distinct parts
give distinct vertices, so the lifted sequence is a monochromatic directed
path from v to y.

There is no concatenation of an internal path of one colour with a quotient
path of a different colour: the outside-target path starts directly at v.
Only B_i needs an internal monosource; no internal hypothesis on the other
parts is used. This proves the lemma.

A useful variant: if i lies on a monochromatic directed cycle of Q, every
vertex of B_i can reach every other vertex of B_i by lifting that cycle.
Consequently, if i is also a monosource of Q, every vertex of B_i is a
monosource of T even without an internal-monosource assumption. The cycle
is chosen simple, and its starting and ending representatives are the two
specified distinct vertices of B_i.

## Lemma 2: inheritance of the forbidden pattern

If T has no rainbow directed triangle, neither any induced part nor Q has one.
The induced assertion is immediate from its unchanged arcs. For the quotient,
choose a representative from each of the three parts of a purported rainbow
directed triangle. Cross-arc uniformity produces a rainbow directed triangle
in T with the same directions and colours, a contradiction.

The quotient still uses colours from the original three-element palette.
It need not use at most two of them. C02 demonstrates that this distinction
is essential.

## Lemma 3: a smallest nonempty root counterexample has no proper module

A colour-direction module M is a nonempty subset for which each exterior
vertex has a single colour and a single direction on its arcs to M.
Suppose T has no rainbow directed triangle and no monosource, and its order
is smallest among NONEMPTY tournaments with these two properties.

There cannot be a module M with 2 <= |M| <= |T|-1. Indeed, the partition
consisting of M and every exterior vertex as its own singleton has the genuine
uniform quotient Q of Lemma 1. Both |Q|=|T|-|M|+1 and |M| are positive and
strictly smaller than |T|. By Lemma 2 they have no forbidden triangle, so by
minimality Q and T[M] have monosources. Every singleton is its own monosource.
Choose the internal monosource of whichever part is the quotient monosource;
Lemma 1 then gives a monosource of T, contradiction.

This proves only a necessary condition. It does not claim that all tournaments
have proper modules, or that every module-free tournament has a monosource.

## Lemma 4: a smallest nonempty root counterexample is strongly connected

The strongly connected components of a tournament form a transitive tournament.
Here is the needed argument. If arcs ran both ways between two components,
internal directed paths would connect those components in both directions;
they would be one component. Thus every component pair has one common direction.
A directed cycle of components would likewise merge its components, so the
quotient is acyclic. A finite acyclic tournament is a total order (a topological
ordering has every arc forward). Its first component S therefore sends an arc
from every vertex of S to every vertex outside S.

If T were not strongly connected, S would be a smaller nonempty induced
tournament with no rainbow directed triangle. Minimality supplies a monosource
v of T[S]. It reaches each exterior target by a single direct arc v->y.
Those arc colours need not match each other or the internal path colours.
Hence v is a monosource of T, contradiction.

This argument does not pretend that S is a colour-direction module: only its
uniform OUTWARD direction is used for the one-edge paths to exterior targets.

## Lemma 5: no nonempty root counterexample has at most four vertices

This small-order assertion in fact allows any palette, provided no directed
triangle has three distinct colours.

For orders 1 and 2 choose the sole vertex or the tail of the sole arc.
For order 3, a transitive orientation has a vertex pointing directly to both
others. In a directed 3-cycle, two of the three arcs share a colour; these are
consecutive along the cycle and their starting vertex reaches the next two
vertices using the one- and two-edge paths of that colour.

For a non-strong tournament on four vertices, its first strong component has
size at most three. Use the preceding result inside that component and its
direct arcs to exterior targets, as in Lemma 4.

It remains to treat an arbitrary strongly connected four-vertex tournament.
Every outdegree is 1 or 2, and their sum is 6; exactly two vertices have each
degree. Name the degree-two vertices 0,1 with 0->1. Vertex 1 must point to both
remaining vertices. Name those 2,3 so that 0->2 and 3->0. Vertex 2 already loses
to 0 and 1 and has positive outdegree, so 2->3. Thus the exact orientation is

0->1, 0->2, 3->0, 1->2, 1->3, 2->3.

Write p=c(0->1), q=c(0->2), r=c(3->0), s=c(1->2),
t=c(1->3), u=c(2->3). The value s will not be needed.

- If p=t, vertex 0 reaches 3 via 0->1->3 and reaches 1 and 2 directly.
- If q=u, vertex 0 instead reaches 3 via 0->2->3.
- If t=r, vertex 1 reaches 0 via 1->3->0 and reaches 2 and 3 directly.

In each case the displayed two-edge path is monochromatic and all other
targets have one-edge paths. In the remaining case p!=t, q!=u and t!=r.
The directed triangle 0->1->3->0 is not rainbow; its colours p,t,r and the
inequalities force p=r. The directed triangle 0->2->3->0 is not rainbow;
since q!=u, its third colour r equals either q or u.

If r=q, vertex 3 reaches 0,1,2 by r-coloured paths 3->0,
3->0->1, 3->0->2. If r=u, vertex 2 reaches 3,0,1 along the r-coloured
path 2->3->0->1 and its prefixes. These exhaust the cases.

## Combined necessary conditions and next search domain

If a smallest nonempty counterexample to the contract's no-rainbow implication
exists, it has n >= 5, is strongly connected, and has no proper nonsingleton
colour-direction module. This follows from Lemmas 3-5, not from the false
directed Gallai partition. Module-free is necessary, not sufficient.

The lifting lemma is a valid conditional replacement for one step of the
proposed induction. The missing universal step is existence or control of
the smaller quotient; C01-C03 show why the requested two-colour partition
cannot provide it. No complete proof of the root is supplied.
