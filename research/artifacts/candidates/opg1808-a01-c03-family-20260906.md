## Frozen scope and new question

C01 and C02 supply small counterexample candidates to the universal
decomposition and to its stronger strongly-connected, underlying-Gallai repair.
This step asks whether removing finitely many small orders could rescue either
assertion. The construction below supplies a witness of each order n >= 4.

Parts are nonempty, with 2 <= k <= n; singleton parts are allowed. The root
still asks for paths whose individual colours may depend on their target.
No root counterexample, verification receipt or ledger change is claimed.

## Construction for all positive integers m

Let X = {x_1,...,x_m}, disjoint from {1,2,3}, and V = X union {1,2,3}.
For i < j put x_i -> x_j, coloured R. For every x in X put

- x -> 1, coloured R;
- x -> 2, coloured R;
- 3 -> x, coloured R.

The remaining arcs are 1 -> 2 (B), 1 -> 3 (B), and 2 -> 3 (G).
These rules assign exactly one directed arc to each unordered pair of distinct
vertices. All three colours occur. The order is n=m+3. At m=1 this is C02.

## Strong connectivity and forbidden-pattern audit

Every vertex other than 3 has a directed path to 3: use x->1->3 for x in X,
and the direct arcs for 1 and 2. Conversely, 3 reaches every x directly and
reaches 1 and 2 via any x in the nonempty set X. Concatenation gives a directed
walk between any ordered pair; deleting repeated segments yields a path.
Thus the tournament is strongly connected.

Every triple falls in exactly one of the following cases.

| Vertices from X | Colours of the three underlying edges |
| --- | --- |
| 3 | R,R,R |
| 2 | R,R,R |
| 1 | R,R,B or R,R,G |
| 0 | B,B,G |

Cases with more X vertices than available are simply absent. No underlying
triangle is rainbow. In particular no directed triangle is rainbow.

## Necessary block condition

A colour-direction module M has, for every exterior vertex z, a single colour
and a single direction on all arcs between z and M. Both may depend on z.
Every part of a valid partition is a proper such module, by applying the
cross-part condition to the part that contains z.

An exterior vertex that sees two members of M differently must itself belong
to M; otherwise the module condition fails. We use this as a forcing rule.

## Forcing certificate: no proper block mixes X with {1,2,3}

First consider a module M containing two members of {1,2,3}.

1. If 1 and 2 belong to M, then 3 must belong to M, because the arcs 1->3
   and 2->3 have colours B and G.
2. If 1 and 3 belong to M, every x in X must belong to M, because x->1 and
   3->x have opposite directions relative to x. Since X is nonempty, a vertex
   x now lies in M. If 2 were exterior, x->2 and 2->3 would split M, so 2
   also belongs to M.
3. If 2 and 3 belong to M, every x in X must belong to M, because x->2 and
   3->x have opposite directions relative to x. If 1 were exterior, x->1
   and 1->2 would split M, so 1 also belongs to M.

In each case M is all of V (case 1 proceeds to case 2).

Now suppose x in X and one member of {1,2,3} belong to M.

- If x and 1 belong to M, the arcs 3->x and 1->3 force 3 into M.
- If x and 2 belong to M, the arcs x->1 and 1->2 force 1 into M.
- If x and 3 belong to M, the arcs x->1 and 1->3 force 1 into M.

Each reduces to a previous case and forces M=V. Therefore every proper
nonsingleton module is contained in X. This is only a necessary classification;
there is no claim that every subset of X is a module.

## Failure of every proposed partition

In a partition into at least two parts all parts are proper. The module
classification forces {1}, {2} and {3} to be singleton parts, with every
remaining part contained in X. The quotient consequently contains a B arc
1->2, a G arc 2->3, and an R arc from any nonempty X-part to {1}.
Thus its cross-part palette has at least three colours.

This contradicts the at-most-two-colours conclusion regardless of how X is
partitioned. The all-red internal orientation of X was chosen for definiteness;
the argument uses only its monochromatic colouring and the specified cross-arcs.

Orders 5, 6 and 7 are obtained by m=2,3,4. These are constructive witnesses,
not the result of enumerating all orientations or colourings at those orders.
Together with C01 this gives a specified partition obstruction at every order
n >= 3. At n >= 4 the witnesses are strongly connected and underlying-Gallai.

## Root scope

Vertex 3 reaches every x in X by its R arc. For any fixed x_1 it reaches 1 and 2
by R paths 3->x_1->1 and 3->x_1->2. Hence every member of this family has an
R-monosource. The family cannot supply counterexamples to the frozen root.
