## New bounded question after C01

C01 defeats the original decomposition using a transitive rainbow triple.
This candidate asks whether the decomposition can be rescued by requiring
BOTH strong connectivity and the absence of every underlying rainbow triangle.
These are explicit stronger hypotheses, not a change to the frozen root.
The witness below satisfies both and still defeats the original conclusion.

As in C01, parts are nonempty and 2 <= k <= n; singleton parts are allowed.
Colours are R, B and G. Only finite-digraph basics and finite combinatorics
are used. Neither the root nor any ledger is updated.

## Complete witness

V = {0,1,2,3}. The six arcs are exactly:

| Tail | Head | Colour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 1 | R |
| 0 | 2 | R |
| 3 | 0 | R |
| 1 | 2 | B |
| 1 | 3 | B |
| 2 | 3 | G |

There is exactly one arc for each unordered pair. The directed cycle
0->1->2->3->0 visits every vertex. Following that cycle gives a directed
path between any ordered pair of distinct vertices, so T is strongly connected.

## Forbidden-pattern certificate

The following table covers all four underlying triples.

None uses three colours. Consequently no directed triangle is rainbow either.
No programmatic enumeration is needed for this four-row certificate.

## Necessary condition on a partition block

Call M a colour-direction module when for each x outside M, the arcs between
x and M all have the same colour and all point the same way relative to x.
The common colour and direction may depend on x.

Every part of a partition satisfying the target's pairwise cross-arc
condition is such a module: choose the part containing x and apply that
cross-arc condition to it and M. In a partition with at least two parts,
every part is proper. It is therefore enough to exclude all proper modules
of size at least two, then test the discrete partition.

## Complete module obstruction certificate

For each of the six two-element subsets, one exterior vertex distinguishes
its two members in colour or direction.

| Proposed M | Exterior x | Two arcs witnessing failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| {0,1} | 3 | 3->0 and 1->3 have opposite directions relative to x. |
| {0,2} | 1 | 0->1 and 1->2 have opposite directions relative to x. |
| {0,3} | 1 | 0->1 and 1->3 have opposite directions relative to x. |
| {1,2} | 3 | 1->3 is B and 2->3 is G. |
| {1,3} | 0 | 0->1 and 3->0 have opposite directions relative to x. |
| {2,3} | 0 | 0->2 and 3->0 have opposite directions relative to x. |

For three-element subsets, the unique exterior vertex x has outdegree
respectively 2,2,1,1 for x=0,1,2,3 in the full tournament. Each is strictly
between 0 and 3. Thus x has both an incoming and an outgoing arc to the
proposed three-element block, contradicting direction uniformity.

This covers six pairs and four triples, every proper nonsingleton subset.
A target partition must therefore consist of four singleton parts. Its
quotient uses R, B and G, and violates the at-most-two-colours requirement.
This proves the claimed obstruction as a natural-language candidate.

## Why the classical partition does not help

The underlying edge colouring does have the ordinary Gallai partition
{0} / {1,2,3}: every cross-edge is R. Its directions are mixed, since
0->1, 0->2, but 3->0. The complete module certificate also excludes every
alternative direction-uniform partition, not just this particular one.

The classical theorem is described, with fixed-version attribution, in
`research/artifacts/source-notes/opg1808-a01-c01-sources-20260906.md`.
The witness and certificate above do not rely on invoking that theorem.

## Minimality and root-scope audit

For n=2 the discrete partition always works. For n=3 under the stronger
underlying-rainbow-free hypothesis, at most two colours appear, so the
discrete partition works. Also, any strongly connected three-vertex tournament
is a directed cycle; its no-rainbow-directed-triangle hypothesis already forces
at most two colours. Thus order four is minimal for a failure within either
the underlying-rainbow-free class (n >= 2) or the strongly connected class
with no rainbow directed triangle, under the stated partition convention.

This is not a counterexample to the root. Vertex 3 reaches every other vertex
using only R: 3->0, 3->0->1, and 3->0->2. It is in fact a stronger monosource
than the root asks for.
