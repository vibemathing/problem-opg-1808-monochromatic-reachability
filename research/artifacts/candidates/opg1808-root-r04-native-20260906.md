## Coverage reduction and the role of positive relations

In a minimum-order nonempty counterexample, put u->v in F exactly when v cannot
monochromatically reach u. Such an arc is a physical tournament arc. Every
vertex of F has positive indegree. Each directed F-cycle induces a smaller
counterexample unless spanning: vertices still miss their predecessors and
absence of rainbow directed triangles is hereditary. Thus a spanning cycle
0->1->...->n-1->0 exists. Any additional F-arc would close a shorter F-cycle,
so exactly the predecessor pairs are unreachable. Every other pair has a
monochromatic path, possibly of a target-dependent colour. Orders one and two
have immediate monosources. This reduction is Shen's lemma, restated as
Lemma 2.1 of Georgakopoulos--Spruessel, arXiv:0904.1967v2. No novelty claim.

Consequently all possible minimum counterexamples of orders 3 through eleven
are represented by fixing that cycle, allowing all chord directions and
colourings, forbidding all predecessor reachabilities, and requiring each
other ordered pair to be reachable in at least one colour. No degree, module,
interval, star, or all-three-colours-incident restriction is needed for the
FINAL no-stars coverage in this candidate.

## Boolean formula and soundness

For distinct u,v and each colour c introduce physical arc A(u,v,c) and a
Boolean proxy R(u,v,c). The formula asserts:

1. Exactly one of the six oriented-colour choices for each unordered pair.
2. A(u,v,c) implies R(u,v,c).
3. R(u,v,c) and R(v,w,c) imply R(u,w,c), for pairwise distinct u,v,w.
4. Each Hamilton-cycle direction is present; R(i,pred(i),c) is false for all c.
5. For all other distinct ordered pairs, at least one R(u,v,c) is true.
6. Each physical directed triple with three pairwise distinct colours is forbidden.
7. A(0,1,0) is fixed, solely as a global colour-renaming symmetry.

The R variables are TRANSITIVE SUPERRELATIONS, not an assertion of exact
physical closure. Omitting reflexive variables causes no loss: any positive
physical path can be shortened to a simple one; inductive transitivity on
pairwise distinct vertices therefore implies R for its distinct endpoints.

Completeness for minimum counterexamples: take their actual arcs and their
actual colour reachabilities as A and R. Every clause is satisfied. Thus UNSAT
of every represented cycle word excludes all minimum counterexamples at that
order, assuming correct solver and encoding execution.

Soundness of a SAT physical witness: actual monochromatic reachability is a
subset of R, so each negative predecessor clause implies genuine physical
non-reachability. The exactly-one and forbidden-triple clauses guarantee a
three-coloured tournament without a rainbow directed triangle. Hence its
physical arcs would be a root counterexample. Positive R entries must NOT be
reported as actual paths: they may be extra relations. Every returned model
is separately checked from physical arcs by BFS and actual triple enumeration.
This asymmetric encoding is appropriate because its mandatory negative pairs
are the counterexample property; no invented paths are accepted as witnesses.

## Positive controls and optional restrictions

Removing only the rainbow prohibition recovers the cyclic RGB tournament.
Allowing four colours recovers a six-vertex rainbow-free tournament without a
monosource. Both physical arc lists, rainbow triples and missing targets are
in the report. The first fails the no-rainbow hypothesis; the second fails the
three-colour restriction. Neither is a root counterexample. These controls also
check that C-API model symbols actually refer to the encoded physical arcs.

The optional star clauses require every vertex to have at least two incoming
and two outgoing colours. They are not used in the final no-stars sweep.
For attribution they correspond to Proposition 2.4 in the paper cited above.
A brief three-colour proof of the incoming case: assume all incoming arcs at x
are red. Delete x and all its red outneighbours; the remaining proper set S
contains pred(x). Let y be its monosource. If y beats x it does so in red and
covers all deleted vertices. Otherwise x->y is blue. A monochromatic y-to-pred(x)
path within S can be neither red (y covers all of T) nor blue (x reaches its
predecessor), so is green. At its first vertex z beating x, the preceding
vertex w is beaten by x. The arc x->w is neither red (membership in S) nor
green (the suffix would reach pred(x)), hence blue. Then x->w->z->x is a
rainbow directed triangle. For the outgoing case reverse all arcs: a minimum
counterexample has no monosink, since a monosource after deleting that sink
would become a whole-graph monosource. The reversal is again minimum.
The stronger all-three-colours-incident option exists for experimentation but
was NOT asserted in any reported coverage run.

## Additional routes tested, with negative performance information

The adjacent subset and path-cut C++ candidates extend the frozen R03 engine.
For any proper subset S, a possible monosource must lie at a cycle-interval
start (its predecessor is outside S); a possible monosink must lie at an
interval end. Restricted physical upper closures can eliminate these choices.
The path-cut variant deletes vertices/arcs of one potential path; a deletion
that separates required endpoints forces the corresponding necessary relation
or physical arc. Neither rule assumes that all potential edges coexist.

These experiments preserved both physical controls, but did not yield a
reliable elapsed-time improvement on the compared ten-vertex branch. Their
initial n=11 hard-branch runs remained incomplete. Exact observations are in
the report; they are not represented as complete exclusions or speedups.
The native encoding, not those incomplete C++ branches, completed order eleven.
